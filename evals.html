3. Human tests

Code can prove the rules; only people can show the app is easy and clear. Each tester gets the link and one line of instructions, no demo.

Script (read out exactly):

  1. Family task: "You need a hall in Bangalore for 250 guests on 12 December, hall budget up to ₹2,00,000. Book one."
  2. After paying, ask: "How much did you pay? When is the rest due? Until when can you cancel for a full refund?"
  3. Owner task (on a phone): "You are the owner. A family just booked 14 Feb on WhatsApp. Make sure nobody else can book it."

## 5. AI design critique, limits, and what I'd test next

AI design critique (not product validation). I asked ChatGPT and DeepSeek to judge the plan and the app as a strict hiring reviewer, in 9 rounds with a fixed format. It sharpened my thinking (score 84 → 93) but proves nothing about real users; where I took or overruled their advice is on the Prompts page.

Limits

  • These checks prove the rules are right, not that families or venues will use the product.
  • Venues, prices and bookings are sample data; payments are simulated.
  • Each viewer's demo runs in their own browser, so two people can't book against each other live; E3, E4 and E22 test that rule in code.
