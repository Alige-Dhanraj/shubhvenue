ShubhVenue — Prompts

I built ShubhVenue with Claude as the builder and ChatGPT and DeepSeek as critics. These are the prompts that shaped it. They are shown in the order I used them, trimmed for length, with a note on what each one was designed to do.

How I prompt

Principle What it looks like in my prompts
Context before the ask Every prompt opens with the problem, the user and the constraint, not just "build X".
Constraints, not wishes Word limits, fixed reply formats, "max 3 changes", "doable in under 1 hour".
Ask for disagreement Reviewers are told to be harsh, to name "one thing I'm wrong about", and to quote exact phrases.
Make it measurable Every review returns a verdict and a score, so rounds can be compared (84 → 93).
Never build before the plan passes Code starts only after a reviewer returns APPROVE.
I decide AI advice is input. Where I disagreed, I said so and why (section 3).

1. The prompts

Prompt 1 · Frame the problem, then plan before building

Problem: booking an event hall in India is painful on both sides.
- Families can't see which halls are free on their date. They call 10–20 venues,
  wait, and visit in person. Listing sites show venues, not availability.
- Owners keep bookings on paper and WhatsApp, answer "is this date free?" all day,
  lose leads and risk double-bookings.
Root cause: there is no reliable, live source of availability. Another listing
site would only digitise the confusion.

Task: write a plan for a two-sided prototype (families + owners).
State: who has the problem, why an app, in scope / out of scope, metrics,
and which assumptions are verified vs guessed.
Do not write code yet. Go step by step and don't overclaim.

Why it works: it names the root cause before any feature, so every later feature has to earn its place against it.

Prompt 2 · An independent judge with a fixed rubric

You are a strict hiring judge for an Associate Product Manager role.
Review the case-study plan below against the brief. Be harsh and specific.
Reply in this exact format:
VERDICT: APPROVE or REVISE
SCORE: x/100
TOP 3 CHANGES (most important first, each concrete and doable in under 1 hour)
OTHER RISKS (max 5)
WHAT IS STRONG (max 3)
=== BRIEF === … === PLAN === …

Why it works: a fixed format makes reviews comparable across rounds, and "doable in under 1 hour" forces advice I can act on tonight.

Prompt 3 · Pick features from a real owner workflow, with reasons

Before building, approve the FEATURE PICK.
Reply: VERDICT, then KEEP / CUT / ADD lists, one-line reason each, max 250 words.

Reference: a venue-management tool has a dashboard, enquiry pipeline, calendar,
date-based pricing, a muhurtam calendar, finance, documents…
Key data insight: one venue has SEVERAL halls (a lawn, an AC hall…), each with its
own capacity and price, and a date can be partly booked.

Questions: Is availability per hall (not per venue) the right call?
Is a small muhurtam marker worth keeping?

Why it works: it gives the model real-world structure to reason from and asks two specific decision questions instead of "any thoughts?".

Prompt 4 · Kill my own first version

v1 makes families "request" a date and wait for the venue to confirm.
That is the same phone call with extra steps.

If availability is live, a family should book like a movie ticket:
pick date → see only free halls → pay a small advance → booked. Rest paid later.

Rebuild around that:
- Login with two roles: customer and venue owner, switchable for the demo
- Customer: date-first search, full cost, book with an advance
- Owner: the booking lands instantly; offline bookings blocked in one tap
Keep it simple, light theme, and make it look like a real product.

Why it works: it states what is wrong with the current product before asking for the fix, so the rebuild targets the cause, not the look.

Prompt 5 · Journey first, features second

Map the journey for families and for venue owners.
For each step write: PAIN -> FEATURE.
Only build features that fix a named pain.
Then judge it: VERDICT, SCORE, KEEP / CUT / ADD (one line each, max 300 words).
Prioritise what best shows product thinking in a prototype built tonight.

Why it works: every feature in the app traces back to a pain on the journey map; nothing is added because it is "nice to have".

Prompt 6 · Invite the strongest disagreement

You are a VP Product with 15+ years in consumer internet.
Be a tough critic and DISAGREE where you think I'm wrong.
Review my plan (instant booking with a 10% advance, two-sided marketplace).
Reply: VERDICT, SCORE, TOP 5 CHANGES, WHAT TO CUT,
ONE THING I'M WRONG ABOUT. Max 350 words.

Why it works: "one thing I'm wrong about" surfaced the best objection to my core decision (a hall isn't a cinema seat), which I then answered in the product.

Prompt 7 · A final fact check, quote by quote

FINAL CHECK before submission. Check this page for factual errors,
claims that sound invented or overclaimed, logic gaps and tone.
Reply: VERDICT (READY / FIX FIRST), SCORE,
MUST-FIX (max 4: quote the exact phrase and give the replacement),
NICE-TO-HAVE (max 3).

Why it works: "quote the exact phrase and give the replacement" turns vague feedback into edits, and it caught an unclear money example and two overclaims.

2. The 5 decisions these prompts led to

Decision I started with What was wrong Trade-off I accepted
Instant booking, not request-and-wait Families "request" a date Same phone call with extra steps Only works if the calendar is truly live, so trust becomes the owner's job
Availability per hall, not per venue One "free / booked" per venue One booked hall hid the others More data for owners to keep updated
Live means live "Calendar updated 16 hours ago" on cards Told families the data might be stale Needs an owner nudge and a ranking penalty
Show the full cost Hall rent only Food, GST and deposit surprise families later Totals look higher than hall-only prices elsewhere
Reviews now, verified reviews later Reviews ranked last Families won't book a venue they can't trust Imported ratings first; only real bookings can review later

3. Where I overruled the AI

AI advice My call Why
Switch to "request to book" Rejected The date + hall is the scarce part; waiting is the problem I set out to remove. Menu and decor are agreed after the date is safe.
Show "calendar updated X hours ago" Reframed A timestamp says "not real-time". Families see a "Live" badge that venues lose if they don't confirm.
Drop business events Kept 3, filtered They only show suitable venues, and they are the first thing I would cut.
Remove "sample data" labels Kept, made subtle Unlabelled sample data would cost trust.
Add WhatsApp intake for owners Accepted It matches how venues already take bookings.
